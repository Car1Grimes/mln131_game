## 4. C-3 — projector confetti

**Reproduced:** sampling every animation frame for 6 s from the first frames of the final screen,
with hit-testing (`elementsFromPoint`; the test temporarily enables pointer events on the pieces —
rendering unchanged): confetti painted **above** the eyebrow/title/podium labels in 201 of 220
overlaps (projector, shared first place), 587/608 (projector, long single winner), 580/601 (phone 360).

animation.

### 12.1 Q3 e7 `scene-stat-source` clipping at 360 px — fixed

**Cause.** At 360×740 the stage is 336×189 px. The e7 statistic (value, a 95-character label, an
85-character two-source line) already reaches FitBox's scale floor (`fit 0.55`) on macOS, where every
text is at its 16 px floor (value 32 px), and ends at 184 of 189 px. The label and source use the **body**
font (`--font-body`), not the display font. The Linux UI font is wider than macOS's, so one more line
wraps: 7 lines of 20.8 px plus the stack's padding and gaps no longer fit, and the last line
(the source) is clipped. FitBox had no lever left between "all text at the floor" and the masked-box
wrap fallback, and that fallback does not help a statistic.

**Reproduced on macOS** (the Linux fonts are not installed here): a 360×740 device with Verdana
(metrics close to DejaVu Sans) and +0.05 em tracking on scene text. On the **unfixed** build the
presentation suite fails exactly as on Linux: `Q3 e7 @ phone360-extrawide … stat-label 16.0px×4,
stat-source 16.0px×3 CLIPPED scene-stat-source OVERFLOW` (103/104, the only failure).

**Fix (smallest maintainable, no text-size change).** FitBox has a new step between the scale floor
and the masked-wrap fallback: if the content still overflows at `fit 0.55`, `.scene-fit-compact` is added and
the box is measured again. It tightens only spacing: stack gap 0.25 rem; the statistic's padding becomes
0.25 rem vertical and 0 horizontal, which also widens the lines; and the line height of body, date
label, statistic label and source drops to 1.2 (from 1.2–1.3). Font sizes and the 16 px floor are
unchanged. The class is applied only when content would otherwise overflow, so laptops and the projector
(fit 1) render exactly as before. Files: `src/components/SceneStage.tsx` (FitBox), `src/styles.css`
(4 rules). No scene content, timeline or media changed.

| system fonts (macOS) | 184 px, fit 0.55 | 161 px, fit 0.55 + compact |
| Verdana (wide) | 184 px | 171 px |
| Verdana +0.05 em (extra-wide) | **205 px — CLIPPED** | 3 + 3 lines, passes |

### 12.2 Q4 transition E2E sampling — made frame-rate independent

**Problem.** The Linux failure was the guard `samples.length > 30`, not an overlap: under load
headless Chromium delivered 15–28 animation frames in the window. A frame-count guard fails on
a slow machine, and a sparse sample can also miss a brief overlap between two frames.

**New check.** The invariant is unchanged and strict: 0 overlapping visible text layers. It is now
backed by two independent records:
1. **Timeline sweep (deterministic).** A MutationObserver runs before the first paint after the exit
   and entrance layers are inserted. The layers' real CSS animations (`getAnimations()`, here
   `scene-exit` + `scene-slide`) are stepped through their whole active time in 4 ms steps; computed
   opacity and visibility are read at each step, and the original times are restored in the same task,
   so nothing is painted. Result: 211 steps over 800 ms. It does not depend on the frame rate.
2. **Real frames.** Every painted frame from 9.0 s until 1.5 s after b3 appears. The window is
   time-bounded, not count-bounded, and starts early, so a late test process still sees the old scene.
   The frame-count guard is replaced by **coverage**: at least one painted frame showing only the
   old scene (b2) and one showing b3 settled (opacity > 0.98, exit layer gone), plus a sweep of
   ≥ 10 steps. If coverage is missing, the check fails with "before false" / "sweep NOT RUN" rather
   than passing.
The "no black" check is kept (≤ 2 empty real frames) and now also bounds the empty time in the
sweep (≤ 50 ms; measured 4 ms). The reduced-motion device gets the same sweep and coverage checks.
